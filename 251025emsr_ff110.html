Executive Summary

On 2025-10-25 between 09:00–11:00, subject Alexander Teggin experienced a tonic-clonic epileptic seizure resulting in significant oral trauma and blood loss. The event occurred during a documented lapse in antiepileptic medication compliance. Subject regained consciousness in bed at approximately 11:00 with no memory of the incident, discovered blood on pillow, and contacted housemate Marlene Botha who summoned Emergency Medical Services (EMS).

Analysis & Interpretation

Injury Mechanism: Seizure Biomechanics

The oral trauma pattern is entirely consistent with tonic-clonic seizure mechanics:

  • Tonic Phase: Sustained muscle contraction produces jaw clenching with bite forces ranging 100-250 lbs (significantly exceeding normal mastication force of ~50 lbs)
  • Clonic Phase: Rhythmic muscle contractions cause repeated impact between upper/lower dentition
  • Tongue Bite: Tongue may be caught between teeth during tonic phase, producing lacerations and additional blood
  • Tooth Fracture: Anterior teeth (incisors/canines) are most vulnerable to fracture from clenching force, particularly if pre-existing dental weakness or previous trauma

✓ No external impact required to produce observed injuries.

Bloodstain Pattern Analysis

Bed Scene Interpretation:

Heavy saturation of left pillow indicates:

  • Face-down or left lateral position for extended duration (estimated 10-30 minutes)
  • Low-velocity contact bleeding (gravity flow into absorbent fabric)
  • Subject unconscious or semi-conscious during bleeding phase

Kitchen Scene Interpretation:

Blood pattern characteristics:

  • Drip pattern consistent with vertical fall from mouth while standing/walking
  • Absence of directional cast-off or impact spatter rules out collision with table
  • Tooth fragment location matches drip zone (expelled simultaneously with blood)

✓ Pattern consistent with expulsion during ambulation, NOT impact trauma.

Spatial Analysis: The "Clean Rug" Problem

Critical observation: The rug at the foot of the bed shows no blood transfer. This has significant implications:

Scenario Prediction Observed Conclusion
Walked to kitchen while actively bleeding from mouth Blood drops on rug Rug clean Inconsistent
Moved to kitchen in postictal state, expelled fragments there, then returned Possible clean rug if blood/fragments already expelled in kitchen Rug clean Consistent
Never crossed rug; kitchen blood from separate earlier event Rug clean Rug clean Unlikely (requires two events)

Most Probable Interpretation: Subject moved to kitchen area during postictal automatism (unconscious purposeful movement), expelled loosened tooth fragments and residual blood while standing near table, then returned to bed with minimal active bleeding remaining.

✗ Rejected (Occam's Razor)

Causality: Medication Noncompliance

The EMS report explicitly documents: "Ran out of medication. Had a seizure between 9am–11am."

This establishes clear causality:

Antiepileptic medication gap → Lowered seizure threshold → Breakthrough tonic-clonic seizure → Oral trauma from clenching → Blood loss + tooth fracture → Postictal confusion + ambulation → Kitchen blood expulsion → Full awakening with injury discovery

⚠️ This was a preventable event. Medication adherence is the primary protective factor for seizure prevention in diagnosed epilepsy.

B. Medical Terminology Glossary

Term Definition
Tonic-Clonic Seizure Type of generalized seizure with two phases: tonic (muscle stiffening) and clonic (rhythmic jerking)
Postictal Period immediately following a seizure, characterized by confusion, fatigue, and possible automatism
Postictal Automatism Unconscious purposeful movements during recovery phase (walking, moving objects, etc.)
GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale) Neurological scale assessing consciousness (scored 3-15; 15 = fully alert and oriented)
Antiepileptic Drug (AED) Medication used to prevent or reduce seizure frequency
Breakthrough Seizure Seizure occurring despite medication, often due to dosing gap or stressor

Known Limitations & Biases

Epistemological Constraints:

  • Subjective Memory Gap: Subject has complete amnesia for incident period due to postictal amnesia. Reconstruction relies entirely on physical evidence and inference.
  • Retrospective Analysis: Evidence analyzed hours after event. Some degradation occurred (tooth fragments dispersed, blood partially dried/cleaned).
  • Single-Person Scene: No independent witnesses to actual seizure. All conclusions drawn from post-event evidence.
  • AI Pattern Recognition Bias: AI models trained on Western medical literature; may not capture region-specific medical practices or cultural contexts relevant to South African healthcare.

Analytical Biases:

  • Confirmation Bias Risk: EMS documentation explicitly names "epilepsy" and "medication lapse" as cause. This strong prior may have unconsciously weighted analysis toward seizure hypothesis. However, physical evidence independently corroborates this conclusion.
  • Parsimony Preference: Occam's Razor applied to prefer single-event explanation over multiple-event scenarios. While justified by evidence, alternative complex scenarios cannot be ruled out with absolute certainty.
  • Medical Model Framing: Analysis conducted through biomedical lens (seizure pathophysiology, injury biomechanics). Psychosocial factors (stress, sleep deprivation, medication access barriers) not explored in depth.
