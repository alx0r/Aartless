01 / exec-summary

Executive summary

SEO and GEO are not rival disciplines. They are two stages of the same pipeline. Traditional search optimisation gets a source technically accessible, crawlable, and indexed; Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) determines whether an AI system can then retrieve, understand, trust, cite, and recommend it. Fail the first stage and the second never happens — an entity that cannot be crawled cannot be synthesised into an answer.

This guide works from that thesis: SEO (technical accessibility → crawlability → indexing → retrieval) feeds GEO (comprehension → entity recognition → evidence → authority → relevance → citation → recommendation) which feeds the business outcome (consideration → trust → conversion → retention). It holds up under scrutiny across the platforms researched here, with one caveat worth stating up front: Google's own May 2026 guidance is explicit that GEO and SEO are not opposing forces, and that optimising for generative features remains rooted in the same core ranking and quality systems as everything else.

What follows is organised as a field guide rather than a manifesto: a ten-layer funnel for auditing AI visibility, a platform-by-platform index of how Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, Anthropic and Microsoft actually retrieve and cite, an evidence-rated table of which content tactics are proven versus mythical, and a working audit checklist and execution protocol a strategist can run against a real brand.

02 / context

Context: the structural transformation

When an AI system becomes the primary interface between a person and the web, the mechanics of what survives retrieval and reaches the reader shift. The question stops being how do I rank a URL and becomes how do I get discovered, understood, trusted, cited, recommended, and accurately represented — inside an answer someone else is generating on my behalf.

Foundational academic work on this problem — including research presented at the ACM SIGKDD Conference by teams from Princeton and Georgia Tech — treats AI-citation visibility as a genuinely distinct information problem from search ranking. The mechanisms an LLM uses to select sources for a generated answer are not the mechanisms SEO was originally built to influence. But distinct doesn't mean disconnected: GEO sits downstream of SEO, and a candidate pool assembled by traditional crawling and indexing is still the raw material generative systems draw from.

Working thesis, stated plainly: SEO → Discoverability → Retrieval → GEO/AI Visibility → Citation/Recommendation → Consideration → Conversion. The rest of this guide tests that chain against platform documentation, structural research, and observed behaviour — rather than assuming it.

AI Visibility measurement protocol

For any target entity: build a corpus of 50–500 representative prompts, run them across the major AI systems, and record — per response — whether the brand was mentioned, whether it was cited with a clickable link, which competitors appeared alongside it, the cited URL, sentiment, factual accuracy, recommendation status, source position, query category, platform, model version, and date. That log rolls up into five metrics:

  • Brand Mention Share — how often the brand is named across the prompt set.
  • Citation Share — the percentage of answers where the brand gets a verifiable, clickable citation.
  • Entity Accuracy — whether the AI describes the brand correctly or hallucinates.
  • Sentiment Delta — recommendation tone relative to named competitors.
  • Source Position — prominence of the citation within the answer.

Prompts should be drawn from a fixed taxonomy rather than improvised — see the full category table in the appendix.

04 / findings

Findings

The SEO foundation that doesn't go away

Generative features — Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode chief among them — lean heavily on retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out: a prompt is decomposed into several sub-queries that hit the traditional index before anything gets synthesised. If a page fails the retrieval phase, there's no generative phase to speak of. Every classification below assumes that starting condition.

The ten-layer GEO funnel

Being indexed and being understood are different problems. This funnel separates the mechanical act of being crawled from the cognitive act of being interpreted, trusted, and ultimately recommended by a model.

  1. 1

    Crawlability

    Can the system physically access the information? Distinct agents use distinct crawlers with distinct rules — see the platform index below.

  2. 2

    Indexability & retrievability

    Once crawled, is it indexed somewhere a query fan-out can reach — Google's core index, Bing's index (which underpins ChatGPT), or a proprietary vector store like Perplexity's?

  3. 3

    Comprehension

    Can the model correctly understand the subject? Unambiguous semantic HTML and clear entity alignment remove interpretive friction.

  4. 4

    Evidence quality

    Fact density — statistics, specific dates, named entities — is one of the strongest drivers of citation probability observed in this research.

  5. 5

    Entity association

    Can the system confidently connect scattered information across different sources to the same real-world entity? Inconsistent naming fractures authority.

  6. 6

    Authority

    Does the broader web independently corroborate the organisation's claims, in publications the AI system already treats as credible?

  7. 7

    Retrieval relevance

    Does the content actually answer the questions people ask — answer-first, rather than the thesis buried under narrative throat-clearing?

  8. 8

    Citation probability

    Is the information structured so a model can confidently extract and cite it — concise paragraphs, bulleted lists, explicit tables?

  9. 9

    Recommendation probability

    Does the entity become a plausible recommendation, not merely a cited source? This leans on sentiment across reviews, forums, and third-party mentions.

  10. 10

    Representation accuracy

    Does the AI describe the organisation correctly? Being visible but hallucinated is a failure state, not a partial win.

Structural feature engineering (GEO-SFE)

Independent of the words themselves, how content is physically arranged on the page measurably affects citation rate. Research into structural feature engineering found a consistent citation-probability improvement from formatting alone — evaluated across three levels:

  • Macro-structure — heading depth and document architecture. 44.2% of LLM citations were extracted from the first 30% of a page; documents that bury the thesis dilute the attention mechanism and lose citation share.
  • Meso-structure — information chunking. Concise, single-topic paragraphs (3–5 sentences) outperform dense multi-variable prose; converting comparison data into an HTML table produced up to a 38% citation lift.
  • Micro-structure — visual emphasis. Emphasis should mark verifiable claims, statistics, and named entities, not decorative adjectives.

Net effect across the structural-engineering research reviewed here: roughly a 17.3% improvement in citation probability from formatting discipline alone — before touching the underlying facts.

Entity optimisation: the agentic web's core problem

As the web shifts from an interface built for human navigation to one built for autonomous agents, entity optimisation becomes the sharpest line between SEO and AI visibility. An AI system is constantly running entity disambiguation — deciding whether scattered mentions refer to the same real-world organisation, person, or product.

A brand named one way on its own domain, slightly differently in a government database, and by an outdated acronym on Wikipedia fractures its own authority before a crawler even finishes the page. Precise schema.org markup, Knowledge Graph presence, and consistent NAP (name, address, phone) data are what give a model the confidence to synthesise a recommendation rather than hedge or omit the brand entirely.

Off-site GEO: where AI evidence actually lives

SEO measured off-site equity mostly through backlink volume. GEO cares about AI evidence ecosystems — platforms models already trust for training data and live retrieval — and a source can carry negligible link equity while still anchoring generated answers.

Technical accessibility: two standards, one confirmed, one debated

Two technical conventions dominate 2026 conversation about agent-readiness, and they are not equally supported.

llms.txt
A proposed root-level Markdown file summarising site structure for AI agents. Google has confirmed it is not required for AI Overviews or AI Mode visibility and functions as neither a ranking nor a visibility input — large-scale studies across hundreds of thousands of domains found no measurable citation lift from its presence alone. It remains genuinely useful for one narrower audience: coding agents and AI assistants (including Claude) that use it to navigate developer documentation quickly. Treat it as a documentation convenience for technical/B2B sites, not a consumer GEO lever.
Model Context Protocol (MCP)
The genuinely transformative standard of the two. By mid-2026, MCP had become the de facto way AI agents integrate with enterprise tools and external data sources, passing 97 million monthly SDK downloads. The July 2026 (2026-07-28) specification moved it to a stateless, OAuth 2.1-authenticated architecture. For B2B and enterprise organisations, a verified MCP server is becoming as basic a requirement as a working website.

07 / discussion

Discussion

Local GEO: a Cape Town example

Take a realistic prompt: "Recommend the best boutique digital marketing agencies in Cape Town, factoring in client reviews and proximity to the V&A Waterfront." An AI assistant answering this doesn't read one website — it synthesises the business as a single connected entity across its Google Business Profile, Apple Maps listing, local directory citations, aggregated review sentiment (HelloPeter, Google Reviews), and its own site. If the website lists an address in De Waterkant while Apple Maps places the business in the CBD, entity confidence drops sharply on the contradiction alone, and the model will favour a competitor with cleaner, consistent data. NAP consistency across every spatial dataset — not just the website — is the actual local-GEO lever.

Ecommerce GEO

For retail, models look for machine-readable variants, live pricing, and availability. A Merchant Center feed bypasses ordinary crawl delay and gets product data in front of generative systems directly. Vague, marketing-heavy descriptions consistently lose to dense, specification-rich ones — an LLM recommending a solar inverter wants voltage, warranty terms, and local stock status, not adjectives.

Adversarial optimisation and GEO spam

As citation becomes a real driver of traffic, manipulation has followed. Unlike classic keyword stuffing, GEO-spam documents are often rewritten to stay semantically natural and human-readable while injecting fabricated statistics, invented expert quotes, or manipulated structural markers designed to match a model's citation preferences — which makes them harder to catch with traditional spam filters. Platforms are responding with defensive rerankers that try to demote manipulated documents before they ever reach evidence selection. For a legitimate organisation the implication is blunt: fabricated third-party praise or synthetic reviews are a policy risk, not a growth hack, and generative-search spam enforcement is active.

Recommendations

The consulting framework

A reusable seven-step methodology for running this as an actual engagement:

  1. 1

    Audit

    Run baseline AI Visibility Scores across ChatGPT, Google AI, and Perplexity using the prompt taxonomy. Establish current Citation Share.

  2. 2

    Entity map

    Reconcile Knowledge Graph presence, Google Business Profile accuracy, and schema alignment across every touchpoint to remove ambiguity.

  3. 3

    Query map

    Expand keyword research to conversational, complex, and intent-driven prompts — not just head-term keywords.

  4. 4

    Content gap & structural optimisation

    Rewrite priority pages against GEO-SFE principles: heading hierarchy, dense statistics, short paragraphs, data tables, direct answers in the first 30%.

  5. 5

    Technical accessibility

    Verify OAI-SearchBot, Googlebot, and ClaudeBot access in robots.txt. Deploy llms.txt for developer-facing sites only. Stand up MCP for enterprise data access where relevant.

  6. 6

    Authority ecosystem

    Shift digital PR from pure link-building toward YouTube, Wikipedia, industry databases, and relevant local directories.

  7. 7

    Measurement & iteration

    Track Citation Share and Sentiment Delta monthly. The underlying platforms shift fast enough that this cannot be a one-time audit.

Executive execution protocol

The critical path, in priority order.

  1. Make the site technically crawlable. Unhindered access for OAI-SearchBot, Googlebot, and ClaudeBot on high-value pages.
  2. Make important information explicit. Tables, bulleted lists, and clear heading hierarchies for fast machine extraction.
  3. Establish clear entities. Harmonise name, address, and product details everywhere the entity appears.
  4. Publish genuinely original information. Firsthand experience, proprietary data, named examples — not commodity content.
  5. Build authoritative third-party corroboration. YouTube, respected publications, Wikipedia — earned, not fabricated.
  6. Optimise for real user questions. Lead with the direct, factual answer.
  7. Make location and product data machine-readable. Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Merchant Center feeds.
  8. Remove unnecessary access barriers. No JS-only rendering, pop-ups, or login walls in front of core information.
  9. Monitor AI citations and recommendations. Run the prompt corpus regularly, not once.
  10. Test across multiple AI systems. Perplexity's structural cues differ from Gemini's and ChatGPT's.
  11. Correct misinformation about the brand. Fix the off-site evidence the AI is actually drawing from.
  12. Measure visibility against competitors. Share of Citation and Brand Mention Share as top-of-funnel KPIs.
  13. Repeat. The agentic web moves fast enough that this is a cycle, not a project.

Reflexive statement

On sourcing and collaboration

This document synthesises two source materials the commissioning strategist supplied directly: an original 26-part research brief and a completed research synthesis covering the same ground. Claude (Anthropic) acted as formatting, structuring, and editorial collaborator under this Forever Format derivative — organising, condensing, and tabulating the supplied research rather than originating the underlying claims.

On what hasn't been independently re-verified

Several specific figures used throughout this guide — citation-lift percentages, the 44.2%/38%/17.3% structural-engineering numbers, the Reddit/YouTube citation-share shift, the 97-million MCP download figure — originate in the supplied research corpus and were not independently re-checked against live platform telemetry or primary datasets at the time of formatting. One academic citation (the 2024 GEO paper) reflects Claude's own general knowledge rather than a fetched source. Treat all figures here as directional, current to the corpus's own September 2026 horizon, and worth re-verifying against primary platform documentation before use in a client-facing claim.

On structure as demonstration

This document deliberately practices several of the GEO-SFE principles it describes — answer-first framing, tables for multivariable comparisons, explicit fact density, a heading hierarchy a machine can parse cleanly. That's intentional: partly practicality, partly a demonstration that the format and the subject matter agree with each other.

Known biases and limitations

  • English-language and Western-platform-centric: Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic, and Perplexity receive far more depth here than regional or non-English AI ecosystems.
  • The framing treats "visibility" and "recommendation" as self-evidently desirable outcomes without interrogating that assumption further.
  • This is a working field guide, not a formal audit of any specific website, and not legal or financial advice.
