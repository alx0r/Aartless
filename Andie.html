// EXAMPLES OF APPROACH — NOT DIRECT WORK, BUT METHODOLOGY

Our methodology involves deconstructing systems, identifying vulnerabilities, and questioning assumptions—applied consistently across all analyses.

Case Study Approach: Population Miscalculation

Recent articles noted scientists underestimating Earth's population without providing specifics. This lack of detail prompted me to calculate it myself.

With approximately 8 billion people reported, and rural populations (43% of humanity) potentially undercounted by 53%, a conservative estimate yields 11.91 billion—exceeding the often-cited 10 billion carrying capacity.

Even assuming a one-third undercount in rural areas brings us to 8-8.6 billion. The real question: Why hasn't this basic math been widely performed? Our frameworks aim to make such critical, reproducible analysis standard.

Systems Analysis: Louvre Heist Breakdown

While media emphasized the drama of the Louvre heist, I examined the operational timeline and systemic failures. Thirty minutes after opening, four perpetrators used scooters and a truck with a mechanical ladder. At 9:34 AM, they cut through a window into the Apollo Gallery; by 9:38 AM, they escaped with eight items.

This wing contains France's crown jewels and the Mona Lisa, yet only 75% of rooms have cameras. This isn't misfortune or mastermind thievery (though skilled)—it's infrastructural deficiency.

My approach: Map timelines, analyze infrastructure, pinpoint gaps, and trace implications (e.g., darknet markets). Like a magician revealing mechanisms over tricks, we expose underlying systems and misdirections.
