RUBBISH ROUTE

How Britain's "recycling" becomes Africa's environmental catastrophe

TOXIC EXPORT WARNING

This is not recycling. This is waste colonialism.

The Oxford Paradox

When illegal waste appeared on Oxford streets in 2025, Britons saw their consumption's physical reality for the first time. This momentary visibility revealed what normally remains hidden: an elaborate infrastructure designed to export environmental crises to Africa.

60,000-100,000 Tonnes of UK e-waste to Nigeria annually

The Oxford incident was temporary. The African dumping is permanent, systematic, and catastrophic.

The Supply Chain

UK Collection & Sorting

Charity donations and "recycling" programs feed into commercial sorting operations. Only 10-30% stays in UK shops. The remainder—mostly fast fashion and obsolete electronics—gets baled for Africa.

Export & Concealment

70% of e-waste gets stuffed into used vehicles (Ro-Ro shipments). Textiles move in 45-100kg bales. The "repairable" loophole in the Basel Convention provides legal cover for hazardous waste exports.

African Processing

Alaba Market (Lagos) and Kantamanto (Accra) become terminal processing points. Workers burn cables for copper, dump textiles in waterways, and face catastrophic health consequences from toxic exposure.

The Human Cost

11-24 μg/dL Blood lead levels in workers (CDC concern: >5 μg/dL)

"We know it's killing us, but it's the only work we have. The smoke burns our lungs, but we need to eat."

— Alaba Market worker

The health impacts are intergenerational: neurological damage in children, respiratory diseases, kidney failure, and cancer clusters in communities near dumping sites.

The Resistance

Organizations like The Or Foundation and Greenpeace Africa are leading the #StopWasteColonialism movement, demanding:

  • Global Extended Producer Responsibility
  • Closing the Basel Convention loopholes
  • Investment in African recycling infrastructure
  • Corporate accountability for waste streams

"This isn't charity. This is dumping. And we're no longer willing to be the world's landfill."

— #StopWasteColonialism campaign
