🎯 Vision: Scenius, Not Genius

This server is Ali's digital newsroom: a calm, small "third space" where shared learning and collective thinking (what Brian Eno describes as scenius) can happen. It is also a research and writing workspace, functioning as a real-time wire of notes, sources, and ideas.

🎨 What is Scenius?

The focus is on collective creativity and community-driven knowledge over individual celebrity or parasocial worship. Scenius emphasizes the intelligence and intuition of a whole cultural scene rather than celebrating individual creators.

Scenius is the communal form of genius—the ecology of support, collaboration, and mutual inspiration that allows great work to emerge from communities, not just individuals.

🧵 How to Use Threads (Essential!)

Threads are your primary tool for organizing deeper conversations and preventing valuable content from getting lost in busy channels. Think of them as mini-forums within channels.

When to Create a Thread:

  • Sharing something long, detailed, or valuable
  • Deep thoughts, research findings, or resource collections
  • Creating "rabbit holes" for unpacking complex ideas
  • When your discussion will span multiple messages
  • When you want to keep a conversation organized and findable

Why Use Threads:

  • Prevents important messages from disappearing in active chat
  • Makes it easy for Ali and others to find and reference discussions later
  • Keeps main channels tidy and organized
  • Creates a searchable archive of research and ideas
  • Allows focused conversations without interrupting main chat
💡 Thread Best Practice

Anyone can create threads—don't be shy! To create one, hover over any message and click the "Create Thread" icon, or use the # button at the top of a channel. Give your thread a descriptive name so others can find it easily.

A video demonstration is available in server announcements showing exactly how to create and manage threads.

🚀 Engagement Strategies: Building Thriving Conversations

Creating a vibrant community requires intentional design and active cultivation. This section provides strategic frameworks for members and moderators to increase meaningful engagement and build scenius.

🎨 The Scenius Activation Framework

To foster collective intelligence, we employ several strategic engagement mechanisms. These aren't rigid rules—they're patterns that encourage collaboration.

1. Thread-First Architecture

Threads transform ephemeral chat into persistent knowledge repositories.

For Members:

  • Create threads generously: When sharing research, start a thread. Don't wait for perfection.
  • Use descriptive titles: "Gaza Media Coverage Analysis 2024" not "thoughts"
  • Cross-reference threads: Link related discussions using message links
  • Follow threads you contribute to: Get notified of replies and keep conversations going

For Moderators:

  • Model thread usage: Start threads for substantive topics to demonstrate best practices
  • Pin important threads: Highlight exemplary research threads
  • Create thread archives: Compile best threads monthly in a dedicated channel
2. Research-Driven Participation

The newsroom model prioritizes evidence-based contributions over hot takes.

Quality Contribution Markers:

✅ High-Quality
  • Primary source citations and links
  • Contextualized analysis with historical framing
  • Cross-referenced information from multiple sources
  • Thoughtful synthesis rather than reactive commentary
  • Follow-up questions that deepen investigation
  • Acknowledgment of limitations and uncertainty
⚠️ Low-Quality
  • "Trust me bro" assertions without evidence
  • Hot takes without research or context
  • Sharing headlines without reading articles
  • Reactive emotional responses to news
  • Repeating talking points without verification
  • Treating opinions as facts
3. Prompting Collective Intelligence

Strategic prompts can activate scenius moments and spark collaborative research.

Effective Engagement Prompts:

  • "What are you researching this week?" – Regular check-ins create accountability and cross-pollination of ideas
  • "Share your rabbit hole" – Invite members to document their deep dives and unusual connections
  • "What's missing from this story?" – Crowd-source gaps in mainstream narratives
  • "Connect the dots" – Encourage synthesis across seemingly unrelated topics
  • "Primary source challenge" – Ask members to find and share original documents, not summaries
  • "Devil's advocate time" – Request the strongest counter-argument to prevailing views
  • "Historical parallel?" – Draw connections between current events and past patterns
4. Recognition Without Celebrity

Recognize contributions while maintaining the scenius ethos—celebrate research, not personalities.

Healthy Recognition Practices:

  • Highlight Research, Not Personalities: "This thread on X has excellent primary sources" rather than "User Y is amazing"
  • Create Research Digests: Weekly or monthly compilations of the best threads and findings
  • Cross-Platform Attribution: When community research informs Ali's content, acknowledge it collectively
  • Avoid Leaderboards: Don't gamify participation with message counts or rankings—quality matters, not quantity
  • Feature Anonymous Contributions: Sometimes share great insights without attributing them to specific users

🚫 Anti-Patterns: What to Avoid

Understanding what not to do is as important as knowing best practices.

❌ Anti-Pattern (Degrades Community) ✅ Better Approach (Builds Scenius)
Heated real-time debates in main channels Thread-based discussions with time to research
"Hot take" culture and performative posting Evidence-first analysis with cited sources
Attention-seeking behavior and main character syndrome Contribution-seeking behavior focused on collective knowledge
Flooding channels with short, rapid-fire messages Consolidated, thoughtful messages in threads
Parasocial dynamics and hero worship Peer-to-peer knowledge sharing and collaboration
Treating the server as a social media feed Treating the server as a research workspace
Demanding Ali's attention or validation Contributing to collective research Ali can draw from

📊 Measuring Healthy Engagement

Unlike traditional metrics, scenius-driven communities measure different success indicators:

📈 Healthy Metrics
  • Thread Depth: Number of substantive responses per thread
  • Source Density: Percentage of messages that include citations
  • Cross-Pollination: How often members reference each other's research
  • Knowledge Synthesis: Frequency of "breakthrough" moments
  • Deep Contributor Retention: Long-term engagement of research-focused members
⚠️ Unhealthy Metrics
  • Raw Message Count: Volume without substance
  • User Rankings: Creates competition instead of collaboration
  • Reaction Farming: Optimizing for likes instead of insight
  • Engagement Rate: Can reward low-quality, reactive content
  • Daily Active Users: Presence doesn't equal contribution
⚡ Engagement Warning

High message volume does NOT equal healthy engagement. A quiet server with deep, researched threads is healthier than a chaotic server with hundreds of shallow messages. Optimize for insight density, not activity metrics.

🚨 Critical Understanding

A failure to comprehend and adhere to Discord's platform governance exposes a community and its members to platform-level enforcement actions, including account and server suspension. Server rules cannot contradict Discord's policies.

📜 The Discord Compact: Two-Tiered Governance

Every user operates within two layers of governance:

⚖️ Terms of Service (ToS)

The legally binding contract governing platform use. Establishes absolute minimum requirements.

Key Mandates:

  • Age: Must be 13+ and meet digital consent age in your country
  • Account Responsibility: You're responsible for all account activity
  • No Account Transfer: Selling/lending accounts is prohibited
  • No Platform Harm: No cheats, hacks, scraping, or DDoS attacks
  • No Illegal Activity: Don't use Discord to plan or commit crimes
🛡️ Community Guidelines

Behavioral standards defining acceptable content and conduct. Used by Trust & Safety for moderation.

Core Themes:

  • Respect & Safety: No harassment, threats, or doxxing
  • Hateful Conduct: No attacks based on protected characteristics
  • Child Safety: Zero tolerance for CSAM or grooming
  • Authenticity: No misinformation causing harm, impersonation, or spam
  • Intellectual Property: Respect copyright and DMCA

⚠️ Discord Warning System

Discord employs a progressive disciplinary system that considers violation severity:

Account Status Tiers

  1. All Good: No active violations
  2. Limited: Temporary loss of some features (e.g., can't post images)
  3. Very Limited: More extensive restrictions for longer duration
  4. At Risk: Further violations may result in permanent suspension
  5. Permanent Suspension: Complete loss of Discord access
🚨 Severity Override

A single egregious violation (especially concerning child safety) can bypass all lower tiers and result in immediate permanent suspension. This is NOT a "three strikes" system.

🏗️ From Guidelines to Guardrails: Crafting Server Rules

Server rules must harmonize with Discord's policies while creating specific cultural norms. This is an act of social architecture.

Technical vs. Cultural Rules

The most effective rule sets build a specific type of social environment:

Technical Rules (Platform Requirements):

  • "No spam" - enforces functionality
  • "No harassment" - platform requirement
  • "No illegal content" - legal compliance

Cultural Rules (Community Values):

  • "No short-breath messages" - cultivates thoughtful communication
  • "No elitism" - creates inclusive atmosphere
  • "Evidence-first debate" - promotes research culture
  • "No parasocial boundary violations" - maintains professional newsroom atmosphere

Our approach: We reverse-engineer rules from our cultural vision of scenius. Every rule serves the goal of collective intelligence.

📋 Core Rule Requirements

At minimum, server rules must:

  1. Explicitly uphold Discord's prohibitions (harassment, hate speech, doxxing, illegal content)
  2. Define content expectations (on-topic vs. off-topic, stance on politics/religion)
  3. Establish behavioral norms (no trolling, respect moderators, no punishment evasion)
  4. Set profile standards (appropriate nicknames, avatars, custom statuses)
⚖️ Governance Philosophy

Server-level rules are the first line of defense and cultural curation. Failure to self-moderate effectively invites platform-level intervention from Discord's Trust & Safety team. Thoughtful rule-making is both community management and risk management.

AutoMod, audit logs, and specialized bots

👥 The Human Element: Team and Protocols

Building the Moderation Team

Ideal Moderator Qualities:

  • Active participation: Regular presence and understanding of community culture
  • Mature judgment: Can remain calm under pressure and make nuanced decisions
  • Level-headed: Doesn't take things personally or escalate conflicts
  • Strong rule comprehension: Deeply understands both Discord's and server's rules

Hierarchy Structure:

  • Moderators: Focus on enforcing rules in chat, managing day-to-day issues
  • Senior Moderators: Handle appeals, train new mods, manage complex situations
  • Administrators: Broader responsibilities for server structure and staff management
  • Head of Community Safety (Tom): Oversees all moderation activities and final escalations

⚖️ Enforcement Models

Consistency is the cornerstone of perceived fairness. Choose a model that fits your community:

Model Description Pros Cons
Case-by-Case Highly flexible, tailored punishments based on context and severity Nuanced, can account for intent and circumstances Can lead to perceived inconsistency
Infraction-Based "Three strikes" system with escalating punishments Highly accountable and predictable Lacks nuance, treats minor and major infractions equally
Points-Based Each violation assigned points; accumulated points trigger punishments Balanced flexibility and structure More complex to implement and track
🎯 Our Approach

We use a hybrid model combining case-by-case judgment with documented guidelines. Severity matters: a single egregious violation can result in immediate ban, while minor infractions get warnings with increasing consequences.

🤖 Technical Toolkit: Discord's Native Features

AutoMod: Proactive Content Filtering

Discord's native, automated content filtering system. It blocks harmful content before it's even posted.

Key Features:

  • Keyword Filters: Block specific words/phrases using pre-made lists (insults, sexual content, profanity) or custom lists with wildcard support
  • Spam Filters: Detect and block spam content or excessive @mentions
  • Configurable Responses: Block message, alert moderators, and/or auto-timeout the user

Best Practices:

  • Start conservative, add filters based on actual problems
  • Review AutoMod alerts regularly to catch false positives
  • Use wildcard filters carefully (hate* catches "hateful" but also "Hatteras")
  • Combine AutoMod with human review for nuanced situations
Audit Log: Accountability & Investigation

Located in Server Settings, the Audit Log is an immutable record of all significant administrative actions.

What It Tracks:

  • Message deletions (who deleted, when, where)
  • Member kicks/bans (who issued, reason given)
  • Role creations/modifications
  • Channel edits and permission changes
  • Server settings modifications

Uses:

  • Accountability: Track moderator actions
  • Investigation: Understand what happened during incidents
  • Training: Review decisions with team members
  • Appeals: Verify facts when members appeal

🎯 Moderating High-Stakes Conversations

Managing sensitive topics like politics requires architectural design, not just enforcement.

Architecting Space for Constructive Debate

1. Containment and Consent

Isolate sensitive discussions into specific, opt-in channels. This respects member agency and prevents server-wide contamination.

2. Enhanced Rules of Engagement

Within dedicated channels, implement stricter rules:

  • Require sources for factual claims - "citation needed" is enforced
  • Prohibit logical fallacies - ad hominem, straw man, whataboutism banned
  • Mandate steel-manning - must restate opponent's argument charitably before rebuttal
  • Evidence-first debate - opinions must be supported by data

3. Slowing the Pace: Slow Mode

Discord's Slow Mode is exceptionally powerful for heated discussions. By imposing delays (15-30 seconds) between messages, it:

  • Disrupts rapid-fire, reactive arguments
  • Forces participants to be more deliberate
  • Reduces emotional escalation
  • Increases thoughtfulness and reduces volume

🕊️ The Moderator as Facilitator: De-escalation

When conflicts arise, moderators must act as neutral facilitators, not police. This requires specific skills adapted for text-based communication.

De-escalation Techniques

Core Principles:

  • Remain calm: Your tone sets the temperature
  • Use neutral language: Avoid accusatory or judgmental words
  • Demonstrate empathy: Acknowledge feelings without condoning behavior
  • Practice active listening: Show you understand before responding

Practical Application:

❌ Reactive (Bad):

@user Stop being toxic or you're banned.

✅ Responsive (Good):

I'm seeing this conversation becoming heated. Let's all take a moment to refocus on the topic, not on each other. Remember our rules about attacking ideas, not individuals.

Specific Techniques:

  • Respond, Don't React: Pause and assess before intervening publicly
  • Validate Feelings, Not Behavior: "I understand you feel strongly about this, but personal attacks violate our conduct rules"
  • Redirect and Reframe: "We may not agree on solutions, but can we agree the problem matters? Let's explore that"
  • Strategic Disengagement: "This discussion is no longer productive and is now closed. Please move on to other topics"

🚨 Crisis Management Protocols

🚨 Responding to Raids

Coordinated spam and harassment campaigns

Immediate Actions:

  • Temporarily increase Verification Level
  • Enable Slow Mode in all channels
  • Deploy anti-raid bots
  • Execute lockdown procedure
  • Ban attackers and delete content
🔒 Doxxing & Severe Harassment

Critical violations of Discord ToS

Immediate Actions:

  • Ban offender immediately
  • Delete all offending content
  • Screenshot evidence
  • Report to Discord Trust & Safety
  • Support victim, provide resources
🚨 Escalating to Discord Trust & Safety

Server moderators should NOT handle illegal activity or credible violence threats alone.

When to Escalate:

  • Child safety violations (CSAM, grooming)
  • Credible threats of violence or terrorism
  • Doxxing with malicious intent
  • Coordinated harassment campaigns
  • Any illegal activity

How to Report Effectively:

  1. Enable Developer Mode (User Settings → Advanced → Developer Mode)
  2. Right-click users/messages/servers to copy IDs
  3. Screenshot evidence with context
  4. Submit report with all IDs and screenshots
  5. Preserve evidence locally in case it's deleted

⚖️ The Human-Machine Partnership

🤝 Optimal Moderation Strategy

The most effective moderation is a human-machine partnership:

  • Automated tools (AutoMod, bots): Handle high-volume, low-nuance work (spam filtering, common slurs, link moderation)
  • Human moderators: Focus on high-impact, high-nuance situations requiring empathy, context, and judgment

This division prevents human burnout while ensuring complex situations get the attention they deserve. Machines filter; humans facilitate.

📚 Moderation Resources

For New Moderators
  • Shadow experienced mods for 2 weeks
  • Study the Audit Log to understand patterns
  • Practice de-escalation in low-stakes situations
  • Ask questions in mod-only channels
  • Review appealed decisions to learn nuance
For Experienced Moderators
  • Document your decision-making process
  • Mentor new moderators
  • Contribute to policy discussions
  • Take breaks to avoid burnout
  • Seek peer review on difficult calls

🎓 Conclusion: The Art of Community Architecture

Discord is far more than a simple chat application—it is a sophisticated and modular platform for building and managing complex digital communities. Creating a successful, engaged, and safe server is an act of deliberate architecture, requiring both technical mastery and nuanced understanding of social dynamics.

🏛️ Core Principles for Community Architects

1. Governance is Layered

Server rules exist within Discord's Terms of Service and Community Guidelines. Understanding and enforcing platform-wide standards is the baseline for mitigating risk.

2. Architecture Precedes Engagement

The structure of your server—channels, roles, permissions—is the primary mechanism for shaping culture and guiding behavior. Design spaces, don't just police them.

3. Engagement is a System

Vibrant communities result from interconnected engines: onboarding, recurring events, recognition systems, and reward structures working in concert.

4. Moderation is Partnership

Optimal moderation combines automation (high-volume, low-nuance) with human judgment (high-impact, high-nuance) to prevent burnout while maintaining quality.
