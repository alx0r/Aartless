THE DAY WE SAVED FRIKKIE

A Mall Epic by Duck Rabbit, Brat Strategist & Gen Z Hype Squad Handler

Duck Rabbit ✨

Swifty, Strategist, & Cosmic Emergency Coordinator

Billie 🎵

18, Fire-eyed Philosopher & Eilish Devotee

Wolverine 🔥

19, Phoenix in Vans & Medical Encyclopedia

There Are Mall Days. And Then There Are Mall Days.

You know the kind: overlit, overpopulated, buzzing with the psychic energy of a thousand passive-aggressive shoppers and whatever eldritch corporate committee decided to make the lighting feel like a dystopian interrogation room.

That day? Was one of those. The fluorescent lights were basically doing a seizure TikTok dance in your retinas. You—Duck Rabbit, part-time strategist, full-time cutie, ethically trained by two psychiatrist parents and running on pure instinct—were casually vibing through the gauntlet of artificial light with your two Gen Z soul-spawn.

The Dream Team

Billie: 18, fire-eyed and sharp-tongued, her brain spinning like a hard drive full of memes, philosophy, and surprising emotional intelligence. Named for her devotion to Eilish's ethereal chaos.

Wolverine: 19, tall, soulful, just barely recovered from a spinal surgery that would've wiped out most mortals. Medical encyclopedias in his bones. A literal phoenix in Vans, earning his nickname through pure resilience.

They weren't tourists. They were pilgrims. And you, Duck Rabbit, were their city-guide and low-key existential midwife. You were showing them around like some sassy mall Virgil leading Dante's smarter younger cousins through the neon rings of hell.

The irony? You wouldn't have even been at the mall that day without them. Your neuro-wiring was already buzzing from the lights—the kind that hunt your occipital lobe with seizure demons. But these two radiant, high-IQ chaos disciples had gotten you there, and you trusted them. That meant something.

You were literally laughing about how the lights felt like they were trying to trigger seizures when—

The Moment Everything Changed

Frikkie fell.

No drama. No buildup. One second you're talking about the hostile lighting, the next, a man—silver-haired and trembling—is down. Convulsing. Frothing. Eyes fluttering like radio static.

The mall people? Froze. The security? Ghosted. The normies? Flailing like dropped spaghetti.

But you? You moved.

Not because you're a hero. (You are, but don't get smug.) Not because you wanted attention. (You had it anyway.) Not even because you were thinking.

You were just—doing.

The Save

Your jacket hit the floor like a medic's cape. You cradled Frikkie's head, cleared his airway, turned him gently, protected his dignity like a sacred artifact. Vomit? Handled. Panic? Dismantled. Chaos? Redefined.

This wasn't impulse. This wasn't heroic instinct. This was just natural response—when a man is in need and you know what to do, you do it. Raised by two psychiatrist doctors, you knew not just the medical stuff but the psychological aspects: crowd control, situational awareness, timing, paramedics, panic, and the collective consciousness of worry.

And Billie and Wolverine? Chef's kiss. They didn't run. They didn't scream. They backed you like a dream-team of baby trauma surgeons and undercover angels.

Wolverine held space. Billie watched for paramedics. Together they read your vibe and mirrored it like emotional Jedi. You were the triage lead, they were the action team. None of you flinched.

"It's happened before," she whispered. You locked eyes with her and nodded. "It's happening now. We got this."

Frikkie's partner, bless her, was calm but cracked at the edges. She told you he was fragile—a frightened old man who needed gentle handling, especially around the head. He'd vomited, needed help with his airways and clothes. You became the person maintaining order and calm strategy, but it was absolutely a team effort.

The Poetry of It All

The whole scene—ten minutes? Twenty? Who cares. Time bent itself around you like a ring light from the gods.

You didn't think—you knew. Because this wasn't theory. It was what you were raised into. You knew how to handle seizures, yes—but more importantly, you knew how to handle people.

You knew how to cool a crowd. You knew when to hush and when to bark. You knew how to make someone's worst moment not feel like a stage.

And here's the most poetic part: Wolverine, who helped you? He'd just recovered from major spinal surgery himself. He knew medical stuff better than most medics. The three of you—one person prone to seizures helping another, guided by two brilliant teens who moved like they'd rehearsed this in dreams.

The Real Story

You were trying to be a role model for these two incredible young people, showing them around your city, taking them places, driving them around because you genuinely enjoyed their company—their insane IQs, their maturity, their kindness, their good values.

But maybe, just maybe, this wasn't your story. Maybe it was theirs. Or maybe it was Frikkie's story, and the three of you were his cosmic emergency team, stitched together by light, fate, and a conversation about flickering mall LEDs.

The truth is, they did more for you that day than you think you did for anyone else. You wouldn't have been there without them. They helped you navigate a space that was already triggering your own neurological sensitivities. They were your support system, and when the moment came, you all became each other's.

No medals. No applause.

Just a man who lived. And a crew who'll never forget the day they turned chaos into care. The day three misfits danced with mortality and came out legendary.

The whole day was cosmic—from joking about seizure-inducing lights to actually helping someone through one, from being guided by teens to guiding them through crisis response, from a casual mall tour to an impromptu lesson in being human.

🌀 Dedicated to Frikkie, the man who reminded us all what being there truly means.

🖤 And to Billie and Wolverine, who helped hold up the sky.

💫 And to the beautiful irony that sometimes the people we think we're helping are actually the ones saving us too.

This is a story about the day we saved Frikkie.
But really, it's about the day Frikkie—and two brilliant teenagers—saved something in us too.
