This report examines Christmas, Halloween, and Black Friday through critical media studies frameworks—including Jean Baudrillard's hyperreality and Gilles Deleuze's societies of control—to reveal how algorithms, surveillance infrastructure, and e-commerce platforms have engineered these holidays into mechanisms of psychological manipulation and environmental destruction.

The "wastage" of these holidays is not accidental—it's a structural necessity of platform capitalism. The system requires constant data flow and goods circulation. The "wholesomeness" we seek has been financialized and turned into a data point for predictive algorithms.

🎄 Part I: The Platformization of Christmas 🧠 Academic

In the contemporary mediascape, Christmas exemplifies the convergence of platform capitalism and affective labor. It is no longer a singular day but a "seasonality service"—a distinct temporal zone engineered for maximum extraction.

📦 1.1 The Logistics of Material Discard

The transition from high street shopping to platform ecosystems (Amazon, Shein, Temu) has fundamentally altered the material reality of the holiday. The "unboxing" phenomenon, amplified by TikTok, necessitates elaborate, non-recyclable packaging designed solely for the screen, not for sustainability.

  • Reverse Logistics Crisis: Approximately 15% of holiday purchases are returned. In the platform economy, it is often cheaper to landfill these goods than to inspect and repackage them. This is "waste by design."
  • Food as Simulacrum: The "Pinterest-Perfect" feast drives the purchase of 230,000 tonnes of food waste in the UK alone. The food is purchased for the image of abundance, not biological satiety.
👁️ 1.2 The Surveillance Santa & Control Societies

Gilles Deleuze theorized the shift from "disciplinary societies" to "societies of control." Christmas provides the pedagogical tools for this transition:

"The 'Elf on the Shelf' is a decentralized panopticon. It trains children to accept constant, granular surveillance within the private sphere in exchange for material reward. It normalizes the 'dataveillance' they will experience as adults under platform capitalism."

Furthermore, the NORAD Santa Tracker represents the militarization of wonder—using state surveillance infrastructure (radar/satellite) to "track" a mythological figure, softening the public image of the surveillance state.

☁️ 1.3 The Heavy Cloud: Digital Wastage

We perceive digital interaction as "green," but the Golden Quarter (Q4) creates a massive carbon spike via data centers.

  • Generative AI: 2024/2025 marks the mass adoption of AI for holiday cards and marketing. One AI image generation consumes as much energy as fully charging a smartphone.
  • Dark Data: The flood of promotional emails (unopened) sits on servers, requiring constant cooling, generating "zombie carbon."

🛒 Part III: Black Friday—The Hype Machine ⚡ Vital

Black Friday is the engine room of the platformized holiday. It is a celebration of the Algorithm itself.

Platforms use "Gamification" (Spin-to-win wheels, countdown timers) to bypass cognitive resistance. This triggers a Dopamine Loop (Junkish Enjoyment) where the act of buying provides relief from the anxiety generated by the platform.

🧠 Part IV: Theoretical Synthesis 🎓 PhD Level

4.1 The Death of the Real (Baudrillard)

Christmas is a Third-Order Simulacrum. We do not celebrate the birth of a deity or the winter solstice; we celebrate the idea of celebration portrayed in Coca-Cola advertisements.

"The hyperreal is more real than reality. The 'Perfect Christmas' exists only in the media; our lived reality is merely a clumsy attempt to replicate the simulation." — Synthesis of Simulacra and Simulation

4.2 Affective Labor in the Digital Enclosure

Mark Andrejevic’s concept of the "Digital Enclosure" applies here. The "work" of Christmas (decorating, cooking, gifting) is now performed for the platform. We produce content (photos, videos) that generates value for Meta and TikTok, while we pay for the privilege of doing so.
