Design Evolution

How the dress came to be

Design Concept - Version 1.0

Date: September 22, 2025

Silhouette: Modified Victorian ballgown with fitted bodice, leg-of-mutton sleeves, and flowing A-line skirt with chapel train

Neckline: High Victorian collar with delicate lace overlay

Sleeves: Dramatic leg-of-mutton (puffed shoulder, fitted forearm) ending in lace cuffs

Bodice: Fitted through natural waist, button-back closure with 40 silk-covered buttons

Skirt: Flowing layers of silk organza over duchess satin foundation, gentle gathering at waist

Train: 3-foot chapel train with scattered Alençon lace appliqués

Silk Organza Duchess Satin Alençon Lace 40 Buttons Sage Ribbon

Major Design Changes

October 10 (First Fitting):

  • Raised neckline by 1 inch (more dramatic, more period-appropriate)
  • Extended sleeves by 2 inches (more theatrical effect)
  • Reduced train from 4 feet to 3 feet (easier to manage, still elegant)

October 28 (Second Fitting):

  • Added sage green silk ribbon detail at natural waist (ties in back)
  • Slightly adjusted bust for better fit (taken in 0.5 inch)
  • Repositioned lace appliqués on train for better balance
  • Decided to add delicate blush silk lining (warmer tone, more romantic)

Materials & Choices

Every fabric, trim, and detail selected with intention

Primary Fabrics

Silk Organza (Overlay)

Source: Italian silk mill, Como

Weight: 5.5mm, lightweight and ethereal

Color: Ivory with slight pearl sheen

Yardage: 12 yards

Why we chose it: Creates that floating, bohemian movement Alexandra wanted. Catches light beautifully. Drapes like a dream.

Duchess Satin (Foundation)

Source: French textile house

Weight: Medium weight, structured

Color: Ivory with warm undertones

Yardage: 8 yards

Why we chose it: Provides structure and support for bodice. Has enough body to hold the Victorian silhouette while remaining comfortable.

Blush Silk Charmeuse (Lining)

Source: English textile supplier

Weight: Lightweight, fluid

Color: Soft blush pink

Yardage: 6 yards

Why we chose it: Warmer undertone creates romantic glow against skin. Luxurious against the body. Changed from ivory during Fitting 2.

Lace & Embellishments

Vintage Alençon Lace

Era: Authentic 1920s French lace

Pattern: Delicate floral motifs with corded edges

Color: Ivory, slightly aged patina (perfect!)

Source: Vintage textile dealer in Paris

Application: Scattered appliqués on train, small details at sleeve cuffs, subtle accent at neckline

Silk-Covered Buttons

Quantity: 40 buttons

Size: 12mm diameter

Material: Covered in matching duchess satin

Application: Center back closure from neck to low hip

Detail: Each button hand-sewn with reinforced shank

Sage Green Silk Ribbon

Width: 1.5 inches

Type: Double-faced silk satin

Length: 3 yards (long trailing ends)

Application: Tied at natural waist, trails down back over train

Symbolism: Represents growth, nature, new beginnings

Internal Structure

  • Boning: 8 spiral steel bones in bodice for support and shaping
  • Grosgrain ribbon: Internal waist tape for structure and fit retention
  • Interfacing: Silk organza interfacing in bodice (maintains drape while adding support)
  • Hem: Horsehair braid in hem for gentle lift and shape
  • Bustle points: Two internal loops for train bustle (for dancing)

The Dress Diary

A narrative journey of creation

Chapter 1: The Meeting

Alexandra walked into the atelier on a Tuesday afternoon in September with a folder full of torn magazine pages and a Pinterest board titled "Victorian Dreams." Within the first ten minutes, we knew this was going to be special. She didn't want a typical bridal gown — she wanted something that felt like a character in her wedding story.

"I want to feel like I stepped out of a painting," she said, holding up an image of a Pre-Raphaelite portrait. "But I also want to be able to breathe and dance and not feel like I'm in a costume."

That tension — between historical romance and modern comfort — became the guiding principle of this entire project.

Chapter 2: The Sketch

After our consultation, I spent three days sketching variations. The challenge was honoring Victorian silhouettes (which can be rigid and costume-y) while maintaining the flowing, bohemian spirit Alexandra wanted.

The breakthrough came when I stopped trying to replicate history and started thinking about reinterpretation. What if we took the drama of 1880s sleeves but made them softer? What if we used the structured bodice but let the skirt flow like water?

When Alexandra saw Design B, she cried. "That's it. That's exactly it."

Chapter 3: The Fabrics Arrive

The silk organza came first, shipped from Como in tissue-thin layers. When we unfolded it in the studio, it caught the afternoon light and seemed to glow. Alexandra happened to stop by that day and we both just stood there, watching it move in the breeze from the window.

"That's magic," she whispered.

The vintage Alençon lace arrived a week later from Paris — actual 1920s French lace with the most exquisite floral pattern. It had a slightly aged patina that was perfect. Not pristine white, but ivory with character. With history.

Chapter 4: The First Fitting

Even in plain muslin, the silhouette was stunning. Alexandra stood in front of the mirror for a long time, not saying anything. Then: "I can't believe this is mine."

We made our adjustments — raised the neckline for more drama, extended the sleeves because she wanted that theatrical sweep. When she left, she kept turning back to look at the muslin form on the mannequin.

"I can't wait to see her again," she said, like the dress was already a person.

Chapter 5: Construction

Building this dress felt like sculpture. Each layer added dimension. The duchess satin foundation gave her structure. The organza overlay gave her soul. The lace gave her history.

Hand-sewing 40 silk buttons down the back took three evenings, but it was meditative work. Each button a small prayer for Alexandra's marriage. Each stitch an intention.

Chapter 6: The Second Fitting (Current)

This was the emotional one. Seeing the dress in actual fabrics for the first time. Alexandra stepped into it and we fastened those buttons one by one. When she turned around and saw herself in the mirror, she burst into tears.

"This is the most beautiful thing I've ever worn."

We added the sage ribbon at her waist on impulse — I had it in my trim drawer and thought it might work. When we tied it and let the ends trail down, we both knew it was perfect. That little touch of color, that connection to nature, that bohemian soul.

The dress is becoming who she is.

To Be Continued...

More chapters will be added after each remaining fitting and on the wedding day itself. This is a living story.
