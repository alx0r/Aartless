🚀 Quick Start Setup Guide

You Don't Need to Be Tech-Savvy! This guide explains everything step-by-step. If you can send an email or create a social media account, you can do this. Time required: 20-30 minutes.

What You'll Need

  • An email address (Gmail, Outlook, anything)
  • 20-30 minutes of uninterrupted time
  • A computer or phone with internet
  • Your completed invitation file from Sadie Bosworth Atelier

Create a Free GitHub Account

What is GitHub? Think of it like Google Drive or Dropbox, but specifically designed for websites. It's 100% free, trusted by millions of developers worldwide, and has been around since 2008.
  1. Go to github.com in your web browser
  2. Click the "Sign up" button in the top right
  3. Enter your email address and create a password (write it down somewhere safe!)
  4. Choose a username (example: emily-and-james or smith-wedding)
  5. Verify your email by clicking the link GitHub sends you
✓ Once you see your GitHub dashboard, you're ready for step 2!

Create Your Repository (Website Folder)

What is a Repository? Just a fancy word for "folder." This is where your invitation file will live. Think of it as creating a new folder on your computer, but this one can turn into a website.
  1. Once logged into GitHub, click the + icon in the top-right corner
  2. Select "New repository"
  3. Repository name: Type wedding (or any name you prefer like our-wedding)
  4. Description: Type something like "Our Wedding Invitation"
  5. Important: Select "Public" (this is required for the free website feature)
  6. Check the box that says "Add a README file"
  7. Click the green "Create repository" button
💡 Don't worry about "Public" - this just means the file is technically viewable, but no one will find it unless you share the link. It's like an unlisted YouTube video.

Upload Your Invitation File

What you're doing: Taking the HTML file Sadie designed for you and putting it in your new GitHub folder so it can become a website.
  1. On your new repository page, click "Add file" → "Upload files"
  2. Drag and drop your invitation file, OR click "choose your files" to browse for it
  3. CRITICAL: Rename the file to exactly index.html (this specific name is required)
  4. Scroll to the bottom and click the green "Commit changes" button
⚠️ The filename must be exactly index.html (all lowercase, no spaces). This is what tells GitHub "this is the main page of my website."

Activate Your Free Website (GitHub Pages)

The magic step! This turns your folder into a live website that anyone can visit.
  1. On your repository page, click the "Settings" tab (top right area)
  2. In the left sidebar, click "Pages"
  3. Under "Branch", click the dropdown that says "None"
  4. Select "main" from the dropdown
  5. Click "Save"
  6. Wait about 1-2 minutes and refresh the page
  7. You'll see a green box that says: "Your site is live at: https://yourusername.github.io/wedding/"
🎉 Congratulations! Your Living Invitation is now live on the internet! Click that link to see it. That's your wedding invitation website, and it will stay online forever, completely free.
💡 Save that URL! That's your wedding website address. Share it with guests, add it to save-the-dates, put it on physical invitations—anywhere you want.

Optional: Connect a Custom Domain

Want a prettier URL? Instead of yourusername.github.io/wedding, you can use something like emilyandj ames.com or smithwedding2026.com
  1. Buy a domain name from any registrar (like Namecheap, Google Domains, GoDaddy) - usually $10-15/year
  2. In your domain settings, add a CNAME record pointing to yourusername.github.io
  3. Back in GitHub Pages settings, enter your custom domain in the "Custom domain" field
  4. Wait 10-15 minutes for DNS to update
💡 This step is completely optional. The github.io URL works perfectly fine and many couples use it. Custom domains are just a nice-to-have.
✓ Setup Complete! Your Living Invitation is now live, permanently hosted, and ready to share with your guests. The hard part is done—everything from here is just making updates when you need to.

📅 Daily Use & Updates

The beauty of Living Invitations is that you can update them anytime. Changed your venue? Update it. Want to add engagement photos? Add them. Need to adjust the timeline? Takes 5 minutes.

When Should You Update?

📍

Venue Changes

New address, ceremony time changes, parking instructions

📸

Adding Photos

Engagement photos, venue visits, behind-the-scenes planning

🗓️

Schedule Updates

Timeline adjustments, new events, transportation details

ℹ️

Guest Information

Hotel blocks, dietary options, dress code clarifications, FAQs

How Often Should You Update?

  • Major updates: As needed (venue changes, significant schedule shifts)
  • Minor updates: Weekly or bi-weekly (adding photos, FAQ answers, new details)
  • Final updates: 2-3 weeks before the wedding (finalize all times, addresses, details)
  • Post-wedding: Add professional photos, thank you messages, create a permanent memory

💡 Pro Tip: Treat It Like Instagram

Think of your invitation as a living document that tells your wedding story. Add updates as things happen—venue tastings, dress fittings, countdown milestones. Guests love seeing the journey, not just the final details.

✏️ How to Edit Your Invitation

There are two ways to edit your invitation: the simple way (edit directly on GitHub) and the advanced way (edit locally on your computer). We'll cover both.

Method 1: Edit Directly on GitHub (Recommended for Most)

Navigate to Your File

  1. Go to GitHub.com and log in
  2. Click on your repository name (e.g., "wedding")
  3. Click on index.html
  4. Click the pencil icon (✏️) in the top right that says "Edit this file"

Find What You Want to Change

Use Ctrl+F (Windows) or Cmd+F (Mac) to search for the text you want to update. For example:

  • Search for your venue name to change the address
  • Search for "5:00 PM" to change ceremony time
  • Search for a specific section like "Travel" or "FAQ"
💡 Tip: The HTML might look intimidating, but you're just changing the text between tags. If you see <p>Ceremony starts at 5:00 PM</p>, you just change "5:00 PM" to whatever time you need.

Make Your Changes

Edit the text you want to change. Some common examples:

Changing Times:

Before: <p><strong>Time:</strong> 5:00 PM</p>
After:  <p><strong>Time:</strong> 6:00 PM</p>

Updating Addresses:

Before: Villa Aurelia<br>Via di Porta San Pancrazio
After:  Thornton Estate<br>123 Vineyard Road

Adding a New FAQ:

<div class="faq-item">
    <div class="faq-question">
        <span>Your question here?</span>
        <span class="faq-toggle">+</span>
    </div>
    <div class="faq-answer">
        <div class="faq-answer-content">
            Your answer here.
        </div>
    </div>
</div>

Save Your Changes

  1. Scroll to the bottom of the page
  2. In the "Commit changes" box, write a brief description like "Updated ceremony time"
  3. Click the green "Commit changes" button
  4. Wait 1-2 minutes, then refresh your website to see the changes
✓ That's it! Your website is now updated with the new information.

Method 2: Edit Locally (For Advanced Users)

If you're comfortable with code editors, you can download the file, edit it on your computer, and re-upload it:

  1. Click on index.html in your repository
  2. Click the "Raw" button to see the raw HTML
  3. Right-click and "Save As" to download
  4. Edit in your preferred code editor (VS Code, Sublime Text, etc.)
  5. Save the file
  6. Upload the updated file back to GitHub (it will replace the old one)

Common Editing Tasks

Task What to Search For What to Change
Change ceremony time Search for current time (e.g., "5:00 PM") Replace with new time
Update venue address Search for venue name Replace address text
Add a new FAQ Find the FAQ section <section id="faq"> Copy an existing FAQ block and modify the question/answer
Change countdown date Search for "weddingDate" Update the date in format: YYYY-MM-DD
Update RSVP email Search for "email" in the form section Replace with your email
Change colors Search for ":root" at the top Modify hex color codes

⚠️ Important Rules for Editing

  • Don't delete HTML tags (the things with < and >) - just change the text between them
  • Keep quotes intact - if you see class="something", keep the quotes
  • Test after big changes - make one change, save, check your site
  • Use version history - GitHub saves every version, so you can always undo mistakes

🔧 Advanced Features & Customization

Version Control & History

One of GitHub's superpowers is that it saves every version of your invitation. You can:

  • View all past versions: Click "History" to see every change you've made
  • Revert to an old version: If you mess something up, you can go back to any previous version
  • Compare versions: See exactly what changed between any two versions
  • Track who made changes: If multiple people are editing, you can see who changed what

Collaboration & Multiple Editors

Want your partner, wedding planner, or a friend to help edit? You can add collaborators:

  1. Go to your repository's Settings
  2. Click "Collaborators" in the left menu
  3. Click "Add people"
  4. Enter their GitHub username or email
  5. They'll get an invitation to collaborate

Customizing Colors & Fonts

Your invitation comes pre-styled, but you can customize the look by editing the CSS variables at the top of the file:

:root {
    --primary: #1a1a1a;      /* Main dark color */
    --secondary: #d4af37;    /* Gold accent color */
    --accent: #8b7355;       /* Brown accent */
    --bg: #fafaf8;           /* Background color */
    --text: #2c2c2c;         /* Text color */
    --light: #f5f5f3;        /* Light background */
}

Change the hex codes to your wedding colors. Use a color picker tool to find hex codes.

Analytics & Tracking

Want to know how many people are visiting your invitation? Add Google Analytics or similar:

  1. Sign up for Google Analytics (free)
  2. Get your tracking code
  3. Paste it into your index.html file before the closing </body> tag
  4. You'll see visitor data in your Analytics dashboard

Password Protection (Advanced)

If you want to password-protect your invitation:

  • Use a service like Cloudflare Pages (free) with access controls
  • Or add a simple JavaScript password prompt (less secure, but works)
  • Or host on a private server instead of GitHub Pages
💡 Most couples don't need password protection. Your invitation URL is hard to guess, and only people you share it with will find it.

RSVP Integration

The RSVP form in your invitation currently logs to the browser console. To make it functional:

Option 1: Google Forms (Easiest)

  • Create a Google Form with your RSVP questions
  • Link to it from your invitation
  • Responses go to a Google Sheet

Option 2: Email Integration

  • Use a service like Formspree or EmailJS (free tiers available)
  • RSVP submissions get emailed to you
  • No backend server needed

Option 3: Full Backend (Most Advanced)

  • Set up a backend service (Airtable, Firebase, etc.)
  • Modify the JavaScript to send data to your backend
  • Requires programming knowledge

Adding Photos & Media

To add your own photos to replace the placeholders:

  1. Upload photos to your GitHub repository (click "Add file" → "Upload files")
  2. Or use a free image hosting service like Imgur
  3. In your HTML, find the gallery section
  4. Replace the placeholder emoji with an <img> tag pointing to your photo
<!-- Replace this: -->
<div class="gallery-placeholder">📸</div>

<!-- With this: -->
<img src="your-photo.jpg" alt="Description" style="width:100%; height:100%; object-fit:cover;">

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to code? +
Not at all! The setup process requires zero coding knowledge. When you want to update your invitation, you'll just be changing text (like "5:00 PM" to "6:00 PM"). It's similar to editing a document. Sadie handles all the design and technical setup—you just need to make simple text changes when needed.
What if I mess something up while editing? +
GitHub saves every version of your file automatically. If you accidentally break something, you can easily revert to a previous version. Click on your file, then "History," and you can restore any past version with one click. It's impossible to permanently lose your invitation.
How long does the invitation stay online? +
Forever. GitHub Pages is a free service that GitHub commits to maintaining indefinitely. Your invitation will stay online as long as GitHub exists (they've been around since 2008 and are owned by Microsoft). Even if you want to take it down later, you can—but there's no expiration date and no ongoing costs.
Can I use my own domain name? +
Yes! You can connect any domain you purchase (like younames.wedding or yourwedding.com) to your GitHub Pages site. Domains typically cost $10-15 per year from registrars like Namecheap or Google Domains. The guide includes instructions for connecting a custom domain, but the free github.io URL works perfectly if you prefer not to buy one.
What happens after the wedding? +
Your invitation becomes a beautiful digital keepsake! Many couples add their professional wedding photos, transform it into a wedding photo gallery, add thank you messages, or keep it as a permanent archive of their celebration. Since you own it forever, you can keep updating it or leave it as-is—completely up to you.
Can multiple people edit the invitation? +
Yes! You can add collaborators to your GitHub repository so your partner, wedding planner, or anyone else can make updates. Just go to Settings → Collaborators and add their GitHub username or email. Each person who edits needs their own free GitHub account.
Is the invitation mobile-friendly? +
Absolutely! Every Living Invitation is designed mobile-first and looks beautiful on phones, tablets, and computers. The layout automatically adapts to different screen sizes. Most of your guests will view it on their phones, so mobile experience is a top priority.
Can I password-protect my invitation? +
GitHub Pages doesn't include native password protection, but there are workarounds: you can add a simple JavaScript password prompt, use Cloudflare Pages instead (also free, with access controls), or host on a private server. Most couples don't need this—your invitation URL is essentially impossible to guess unless you share it.
How do I collect RSVPs? +
Your invitation includes an RSVP form interface. You can connect it to: a Google Form (easiest), an email service like Formspree (responses emailed to you), or a backend database like Airtable or Firebase. The Advanced section of this guide covers all options. Many couples also just include an email address or phone number for RSVPs.
What if GitHub shuts down? +
GitHub is owned by Microsoft and is one of the most important platforms in the tech world—it's highly unlikely to shut down. But even if it did, you own the file completely. You can download it anytime and host it anywhere else: your own server, Netlify, Vercel, or even open it directly from your computer. You're never locked in.
Can I see analytics on who visits? +
Yes! You can add Google Analytics (free) or similar tracking to see how many people visit, which pages they view, and other metrics. The Advanced section has instructions. This is completely optional—some couples like the data, others prefer not to track.

🔍 Reflexive Statement

On Design Philosophy

This system exists to empower couples with full ownership and control over their wedding invitations, resisting the subscription-based, vendor lock-in model that dominates the wedding industry. We believe that wedding memories should be permanent, accessible, and owned by the couple—not rented from a platform that could disappear.

This guide was co-created through human-AI collaboration. The human (Sadie Bosworth Atelier) provided strategic direction, brand understanding, client needs, and wedding industry expertise. Known Limitations

  • Technical Comfort Required: While we've made this as simple as possible, users must be comfortable with basic computer tasks (creating accounts, uploading files). Those with extreme tech anxiety may still find this challenging.
  • GitHub Dependency: The free hosting relies on GitHub Pages continuing to offer free service. While highly unlikely to change, it's a dependency outside our control.
  • Learning Curve for Edits: Making simple text changes is easy, but design changes (colors, layouts, adding features) requires HTML/CSS knowledge.
  • RSVP Integration: The RSVP form requires additional setup with third-party services. It's not fully automatic out-of-the-box.
  • Initial Time Investment: The 20-30 minute setup time is a barrier compared to template services where you just enter information into forms.

What This System Cannot Solve

  • Print invitation design and fulfillment (this is digital-only)
  • Guest list management and tracking (requires additional tools)
  • Automated RSVP collection without third-party integration
  • Real-time collaborative editing (GitHub requires sequential edits)
  • The emotional labor of wedding planning itself

Potential Biases

  • Technical Optimism: This guide assumes most couples can learn basic GitHub operations. Reality may vary by demographic and technical comfort level.
  • Design Aesthetic: Living Invitations favor minimalist, editorial aesthetics. Couples wanting maximalist or highly ornate designs may find the format limiting.
  • Western-Centric: The examples and language assume Western wedding traditions and may not fully address cultural variations in wedding celebrations globally.
  • Digital Access: Assumes guests have reliable internet and devices to view digital invitations. May not work for elderly guests or those with limited connectivity.
