What is Encryption?

Think of encryption like a locked safe. Your document goes inside the safe, and only people with the correct key can open it and read what's inside. Even if someone steals the safe, they can't see what's in it without the key.

Encryption is a way of scrambling your information so that only you (and people you choose) can read it. To everyone else, it looks like random gibberish.

📄 Without Encryption
  • Anyone can read your document
  • Service providers can see everything
  • Easy for hackers to steal information
  • Data can be sold or misused
🔒 With Encryption
  • Only you can read your document
  • Service can't see your content
  • Protected from hackers
  • You control who has access

Why Should I Use Encrypted Documents?

🏥 For Sensitive Information

Use encryption when your documents contain:

  • Medical records - your health information should stay private
  • Financial documents - bank statements, tax returns, passwords
  • Legal documents - contracts, agreements, personal correspondence
  • Research data - confidential studies or proprietary information
  • Personal journals - your private thoughts and experiences

🛡️ Protection You Get

✅ What Encryption Protects Against
  • Data breaches: Even if a company gets hacked, your encrypted data stays safe
  • Snooping: No one can read your documents without permission - not even the service provider
  • Identity theft: Your personal information stays scrambled and useless to thieves
  • Unauthorized access: Only people with the right key can decrypt and view your content

🌍 When You DON'T Need Encryption

Not everything needs to be encrypted. Skip encryption for:

  • Public information you want to share widely
  • Documents with no personal or sensitive data
  • Content meant for search engines to find
  • Situations where simplicity is more important than privacy

How Does It Actually Work?

1

You Create a Password

Think of this as the master key to your safe. Choose a strong password that you'll remember, but others can't guess. This password never leaves your device and is never sent to any server.

💡 Password Tips
  • Use at least 12 characters
  • Mix letters, numbers, and symbols
  • Don't use personal information (birthdays, names)
  • Consider using a passphrase: "CorrectHorseBatteryStaple"
2

Your Device Creates Keys

From your password, your device automatically creates special cryptographic "keys." Think of these as the actual physical keys that lock and unlock your safe.

You have two types of keys: a public key (like your home address - anyone can send you mail) and a private key (like your house key - only you have it). People use your public key to send you encrypted documents, but only your private key can open them.
3

Your Content Gets Scrambled

Before anything leaves your device, it's scrambled using your keys. This happens automatically in your web browser or app. The original, readable version never leaves your device.

Original: "My confidential research notes"
Encrypted: "8f3a9c2e5b7d1f4a6c8e0b2d4f6a8c0e..."

4

Scrambled Data is Stored

Only the scrambled version is stored on servers. Even if someone breaks into the server, all they see is gibberish. Without your keys, the data is useless.

🔐 Zero-Knowledge Architecture

This means the service provider has zero knowledge of what's in your documents. They can't read them, sell them, or be forced to hand them over to anyone.

5

You Decrypt to Read

When you want to read your document, you log in with your password. Your device uses this to recreate your private key, downloads the scrambled data, and unscrambles it locally. Everything happens on your device - the service never sees your readable content.

Using Encrypted Documents

📝 Creating Your First Encrypted Document

  1. Set up your account with a strong password
  2. Save your recovery key (explained below)
  3. Create a new document and enable encryption
  4. Write your content - it's automatically encrypted as you type
  5. Close and reopen to verify you can access it

🔑 About Recovery Keys

⚠️ Important: Save Your Recovery Key!

When you set up encryption, you'll get a recovery key - a long random string of letters and numbers. This is your backup in case you forget your password.

Critical actions:

  • ✅ Write it down on paper
  • ✅ Store it in a safe place (like a physical safe or safety deposit box)
  • ✅ Never store it digitally on the same device
  • ❌ Don't email it to yourself
  • ❌ Don't save it in an unencrypted note
🚨 Without Your Password or Recovery Key

Your data is permanently lost. This is the trade-off for true security - nobody can recover your data if you lose your keys. Not the service provider, not a government, nobody. This is a feature, not a bug!

Sharing Encrypted Documents

👥 Sharing with Other Users

When you want to share an encrypted document with someone, the system automatically:

  1. Finds their public key
  2. Creates a copy of the document's key encrypted for them
  3. Sends them access to the document
  4. They can now decrypt and read it using their private key
It's like making a copy of your safe key and putting it in their personal lockbox. Only they can get that key out, but once they have it, they can open the safe anytime.

🔗 Sharing via Link

You can also share encrypted documents via a special link. The encryption key is hidden in the link itself (after the # symbol), so anyone with the full link can access the document.

⚠️ Link Sharing Security
  • Secure: Send links via encrypted messaging apps (Signal, WhatsApp)
  • Risky: Posting links publicly or sending via unencrypted email
  • Best practice: Use link expiration and view limits when available

🔒 Verifying People Are Who They Say They Are

Before sharing sensitive documents, you can verify someone's identity using their verification phrase - a unique set of words generated from their public key.

How to verify:

  1. Look up their verification phrase in the app
  2. Contact them through another channel (phone call, in person)
  3. Ask them to read their verification phrase
  4. If they match, mark them as "verified"
  5. Now you can safely share encrypted documents with them

Frequently Asked Questions

❓ Is encryption legal?

Yes! Encryption is legal in most countries and is widely used by banks, hospitals, and businesses to protect sensitive information. It's a standard security practice.

❓ Will encryption slow down my documents?

Modern encryption is very fast. You might notice a brief moment when opening large documents, but day-to-day use feels the same as unencrypted documents.

❓ Can the government/police access my encrypted documents?

With true end-to-end encryption, even the service provider can't decrypt your documents. Law enforcement would need your password or recovery key. This is why it's critical to keep your keys secure.

❓ What if I forget my password?

This is why your recovery key is so important! If you have your recovery key saved safely, you can use it to reset your password and regain access. Without either your password or recovery key, your data is permanently inaccessible.

❓ Can I use encryption on my phone?

Yes! Modern encryption works on phones, tablets, and computers. The apps handle all the complex cryptography automatically - you just need to remember your password.

❓ Is this the same encryption banks use?

Yes and no. Banks use encryption to protect data in transit (when it travels over the internet). This is end-to-end encryption, which means your data is encrypted on your device and stays encrypted everywhere - even the service provider can't read it. This is actually stronger than what most banks offer.

❓ What happens if the company gets hacked?

Because everything is encrypted with your keys (which the company doesn't have), hackers would only get encrypted gibberish. They can't decrypt your documents without your password and keys.

❓ Can I switch from unencrypted to encrypted later?

Usually yes, but check with your specific service. Generally, you can enable encryption and your existing documents will be encrypted going forward. Some services can encrypt existing documents retroactively.

❓ Is encryption hard to use?

Not anymore! Modern encryption systems handle all the complicated math automatically. You just need to: (1) create a strong password, (2) save your recovery key safely, and (3) use your documents normally. The encryption happens invisibly in the background.

Best Practices Summary

✅ Do These Things
  • Use a strong, unique password for your encrypted documents
  • Save your recovery key in a safe physical location
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) if available
  • Verify people's identities before sharing sensitive documents
  • Keep your devices updated with security patches
  • Use encrypted messaging for sharing document links
  • Log out from shared/public computers
❌ Don't Do These Things
  • Don't use the same password for multiple services
  • Don't share your password with anyone
  • Don't store your recovery key digitally without encryption
  • Don't share encrypted links over unsecure channels
  • Don't ignore software updates
  • Don't use public Wi-Fi without a VPN for sensitive work
  • Don't assume "Delete" means gone - revoke access properly
