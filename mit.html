MITRE: The Cybernetic War Machine

How a "non-profit" became permanent infrastructure for state surveillance and military power

Origins: The Cybernetics Revolution (1940-1953)

Wiener's War Work: The Original Cybernetic Weapon

Before MITRE, before the conferences, before the theory, there was a practical problem that birthed cybernetics: How do you shoot down a fast-moving airplane?

The predictor problem (1940-1942):

  • German bombers move faster than human reflexes allow
  • By the time you see where the plane is, it's already somewhere else
  • Anti-aircraft guns must fire where the plane will be, not where it is
  • Solution: Predict the future from past behavior
WIENER'S AA PREDICTOR (1940-1943): Radar tracks plane position over time ↓ Mathematical model extrapolates trajectory ↓ Servomechanism (automated control system) aims gun ↓ Gun fires at predicted future position ↓ System observes result (hit/miss) ↓ Adjusts model parameters (LEARNING) ↓ Next target engaged with improved prediction

This wasn't just a better gun—it was a new ontology:

  • The pilot becomes information: Not a human being, but a pattern of coordinates changing over time
  • The future becomes calculable: Statistical prediction replaces human judgment
  • The machine becomes autonomous: Servomechanisms adjust faster than human operators can intervene
  • Error becomes signal: Mistakes aren't failures—they're data for improving the model
Wiener's horror: He realized he'd created something profound and terrifying. In Cybernetics (1948) and The Human Use of Human Beings (1950), he warned that treating people as information flows—as targets to be predicted and controlled—would spread far beyond warfare. He was right, and ignored.

The Macy Conferences (1946-1953): Formalizing the Control Paradigm

The war work needed theory. In a Manhattan apartment, mathematicians, neurologists, anthropologists, and military strategists gathered to solve: How do you control complex systems?

Key figures included:

  • Norbert Wiener (MIT): Coined "cybernetics" from the Greek for "steersman"—the art of control through feedback
  • John von Neumann: Computer architecture and game theory for nuclear strategy
  • Claude Shannon (MIT): Information theory—the mathematics of messages and signals
  • Warren McCulloch & Walter Pitts: Neurons as logic gates—brains are computers, computers are brains
  • Gregory Bateson: Showed how feedback loops govern families, cultures, ecosystems
The revelation: Anti-aircraft guns, thermostats, nervous systems, economies, and societies all operate on the same principle—detect deviation from goal state, send corrective signal, measure effect, repeat. This is the cybernetic loop, and it works on anything.

Von Neumann's Escalation: Game Theory as War Science

While Wiener automated the gun, John von Neumann automated strategy itself:

Game Theory & Nuclear War:

  • Two-player zero-sum games: Your loss is my gain—conflict as mathematical structure
  • Minimax theorem: Optimal strategy assumes your opponent is perfectly rational and hostile
  • Mutual Assured Destruction (MAD): The "rational" outcome when both players can annihilate each other
  • RAND Corporation (1948): Von Neumann helps found the think tank that applies game theory to nuclear strategy

This created strategic cybernetics—not just automated weapons, but automated decision-making about when to use them:

  • Early warning systems detect Soviet missile launches
  • Computer models calculate optimal response
  • Time pressure demands machine-speed decisions
  • Human leadership reduced to "launch authority" for pre-planned strikes
The escalation: Wiener automated the gun. Von Neumann automated the general. The next step—fully automated war—was only a matter of time and computing power.

Shannon's Ambiguity: Information Theory's Military Origins

Claude Shannon's A Mathematical Theory of Communication (1948) is taught as neutral math. But it emerged directly from military problems:

  • Cryptography work at Bell Labs: Shannon worked on secure communications for the military during WWII
  • The problem: How much information can you transmit through a noisy channel (radio signals under jamming)?
  • The solution: Treat information as pure quantity—bits—divorced from meaning
  • The implication: Messages become measurable, optimizable, controllable—whether they're orders to troops or conversations between citizens

Shannon's insight enabled:

  • Efficient military communications under battlefield conditions
  • Error correction codes for missile guidance systems
  • Compression algorithms for surveillance data
  • The entire architecture of digital communication—designed for command and control

MIT: The Forge of Cybernetic Warfare (1940-1960)

Where Theory Became Weapons

MIT wasn't just hosting conferences—it was building the infrastructure of cybernetic warfare:

  • Radiation Laboratory (1940-1945): Developed microwave radar—the sensing apparatus for cybernetic systems. 4,000 people, $1.5 billion (inflation-adjusted), 100+ companies involved. This wasn't academic research—it was industrial-scale war production.
  • Servomechanisms Lab: Where Wiener worked on automated fire-control systems—guns that aimed themselves by predicting target trajectories. The integration of computation, sensing, and actuation.
  • Research Laboratory of Electronics: Where Shannon developed information theory while working on encrypted military communications. "Electronics" meant warfare electronics.
  • Lincoln Laboratory (1951): Created specifically to build SAGE—the first attempt to make an entire continent into a cybernetic system. Permanent military lab disguised as university research.

The Pentagon didn't just fund this research—it structured the entire epistemology. Problems were defined by military needs. Success meant operational deployment. "Dual-use" meant war logic embedded in civilian systems from the start.

Project Charles (1951): The Blueprint for Permanent Emergency

In summer 1951, MIT convened "Project Charles"—a secret study on how to defend America from Soviet bombers. The participants included:

  • Jerome Wiesner (later MIT president, Kennedy's science advisor)
  • Jerrold Zacharias (physicist who'd worked on the atomic bomb)
  • George Valley (physicist who'd pushed for continental air defense)
  • James Killian (MIT president who blessed the militarization)

The conclusion: America needed a computerized air defense system operating 24/7/365, processing data from hundreds of radars, automatically tracking thousands of targets, coordinating fighters and missiles—SAGE.

But more importantly: This required a permanent organization that could:

  • Operate outside normal government bureaucracy
  • Maintain security clearances for thousands of personnel
  • Bridge military needs and technical innovation
  • Survive changes in administration
  • Expand to new threats as technology evolved
The birth of MITRE (1958): Spun out of MIT's Lincoln Lab to manage SAGE, MITRE was the organizational form cybernetic warfare required. Not quite government, not quite corporate, not quite academic—a new entity for a new kind of permanent war.

The Chomsky Problem: MIT's Conscience and Complicity

Noam Chomsky arrived at MIT in 1955, just as the cybernetic war machine was reaching maturity. His story reveals the contradiction at MIT's heart:

Chomsky's Funding: Embedded in the War Machine

  • Electronics Research Lab: Chomsky's early work was funded by the U.S. Army Signal Corps, Office of Naval Research, and Air Force
  • Machine translation projects: His revolutionary linguistics emerged from military interest in automating Russian-to-English translation for intelligence gathering
  • Cognitive science: The field itself was largely created by military funding seeking to understand human information processing (for propaganda, intelligence analysis, and human-machine interfaces)

The Contradiction Chomsky Lived

Chomsky became the most prominent anti-war intellectual in America while working at an institution that was essentially a Pentagon annex:

  • 1960s anti-war activism: Chomsky spoke at protests, wrote American Power and the New Mandarins (1969), criticized the "technocratic intelligentsia" serving empire
  • But his MIT salary came from: The same military agencies funding counterinsurgency research, Vietnam War tech, and the surveillance systems he opposed
  • The Instrumentation Lab: Down the hall from Chomsky's office, engineers designed the guidance systems for Minuteman missiles and Apollo moon shots—precision cybernetic control of nuclear weapons
  • 1969 protests: Students occupied the MIT president's office demanding the Instrumentation Lab be closed. Chomsky supported them, but the lab just changed its name (to Draper Laboratory) and kept working
Chomsky's insight about himself: In a 1969 essay, he wrote about the "new mandarins"—technical intellectuals who serve power while claiming neutrality. He understood he was part of the system he critiqued—that his ability to critique required the institutional position that military funding provided. This is the trap: you can't escape the cybernetic enclosure, only understand your position within it.

What Chomsky Revealed About MIT

Chomsky's presence at MIT demonstrates three crucial things:

  1. Legitimation: Having anti-war voices like Chomsky let MIT maintain its image as a place of "free inquiry" while 75% of its budget came from the Pentagon
  2. Compartmentalization: Chomsky could critique U.S. imperialism in his office while engineers down the hall built the tools of empire—different departments, same institution, same funding sources
  3. The impossibility of pure position: Even the most radical critic was embedded in, funded by, and enabled by the military-industrial-academic complex he opposed

The "Cognitive Revolution" as Military Project

Chomsky's linguistics revolutionized the field, but the "cognitive revolution" he sparked had military origins:

  • Cybernetics as precursor: Treating minds as information-processing systems came directly from Wiener, McCulloch, and military-funded research
  • Artificial intelligence: Born at the 1956 Dartmouth Conference, funded by military interest in automated decision-making
  • Computational linguistics: Developed to automate translation of intercepted Soviet communications
  • Cognitive modeling: Funded to improve training, propaganda, psychological operations, and human-machine interfaces for weapons systems
The pattern: Every attempt to understand human minds through cybernetic/computational models was simultaneously an attempt to control human behavior. Chomsky studied language as a window into human nature; the Pentagon funded it as a window into human manipulation.

SAGE: The Prototype for Everything After (1958-1984)

MITRE emerged from SAGE (Semi-Automatic Ground Environment)—the first continent-scale computerized air defense system built to detect Soviet bombers. This wasn't just a weapon system—it was a proof of concept that you could automate sovereignty itself.

23 radar sites across North America ↓ Data transmitted to AN/FSQ-7 computers (largest ever built) ↓ Automated threat assessment and track prediction ↓ Fighter jets and missiles receive targeting coordinates ↓ Human operators reduced to "exception handlers" ↓ System learns from every engagement

SAGE established the template:

  • Total information awareness: Every inch of airspace monitored continuously
  • Machine speed decision-making: Humans too slow for jet-age war
  • Network architecture: Distributed sensing, centralized processing, distributed response
  • Predictive modeling: Don't just track threats—anticipate where they'll be
  • Permanent operation: The system never sleeps, because threats never stop
The hidden revolution: SAGE normalized the idea that automated systems should make life-and-death decisions faster than democratic deliberation allows. This is the original sin of cybernetic governance.

The Cybernetic Logic

THREAT (input) ↓ SENSING (radar, cameras, data collection) ↓ PROCESSING (algorithms, threat models, AI) ↓ RESPONSE (automated countermeasures) ↓ EVALUATION (effectiveness assessment) ↓ NEW THREATS (discovered by the response) ↓ [loop continues, faster each time]

This isn't just technology—it's an epistemology. The world becomes:

  • Detectable: via sensors everywhere
  • Knowable: via processing and modeling
  • Predictable: via machine learning on behavioral data
  • Controllable: via automated feedback loops

The Military-Academic Feedback Loop

By the 1960s, the pattern was set:

  1. Military identifies problem (Soviet missiles, encrypted communications, supply chain logistics)
  2. University receives contract (especially MIT, Stanford, Carnegie Mellon)
  3. Researchers develop solution (cybernetic control systems, AI, networking protocols)
  4. FFRDC operationalizes it (MITRE, RAND, Aerospace Corporation)
  5. System gets deployed (in war, in surveillance, in infrastructure)
  6. New problems emerge (that require more cybernetic solutions)
  7. Loop accelerates

This produced:

  • ARPANET (became the internet)—designed for nuclear-survivable command and control
  • GPS—built for missile guidance, now governs global logistics
  • Drones—cybernetic loop perfected (sensor-to-shooter in minutes, then seconds)
  • Algorithmic surveillance—ECHELON, PRISM, predict-and-preempt policing

What MITRE Actually Is

Official story: A trusted, independent, non-profit research center serving the public interest in national security and civilian infrastructure.

Reality: A permanent government contractor that operates as infrastructure—too embedded to fail, too classified to scrutinize, too "non-profit" to question.

Key fact: MITRE is a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC). This means no competitive bidding, permanent funding, and a mandate that survives every administration. They've been at it since 1958.

Derrida's Trace: The Program as Metaphysics

Of Grammatology and the Cybernetic Age

When Jacques Derrida wrote Of Grammatology (1967), he was watching cybernetics colonize everything. His intervention was radical: cybernetics doesn't just describe reality—it reveals the truth that Western metaphysics has always denied.

Derrida's provocation: "If the theory of cybernetics is by itself to oust all metaphysical concepts—including the concepts of soul, of life, of value, of choice, of memory—which until recently served to separate the machine from man, it must conserve the notion of writing, trace, written mark, or grapheme."

What does this mean? The West has always privileged:

  • Speech over writing: Speaking is direct, present, authentic; writing is secondary, derivative, a mere representation
  • Presence over absence: What is immediately given to consciousness is real; mediation is suspect
  • Meaning over signal: Content matters, form is just the container
  • Human over machine: We have consciousness, intentionality, free will; machines merely execute

Cybernetics inverts all of this—and Derrida says: good, because it was always backwards.

The Program: Writing Before Speech

Consider what MITRE does:

  • Threat models are written before threats appear
  • CVE identifiers are assigned before anyone understands the vulnerability
  • The ATT&CK framework defines adversaries before observing their behavior
  • System architectures are specified before systems exist

This is the program—not a plan you write down, but the logic of inscription that precedes and enables all execution.

Traditional metaphysics: INTENTION (in the mind) → SPEECH (direct expression) → WRITING (recording) Cybernetic reality: PROGRAM (written protocol) → EXECUTION (automated) → "INTENTION" (retroactive attribution) Derrida's insight: The second has always been true. We just pretended otherwise.

The Trace: Nothing Outside the System

Derrida's concept of the trace is crucial for understanding MITRE's power:

A trace is not a mark left by something that was present and is now absent. The trace is the condition of anything appearing as "present" in the first place.

Apply this to cybernetic systems:

  • You only "exist" as data traces: Location pings, network packets, biometric scans, purchasing patterns. There is no "you" prior to these inscriptions—they constitute you as legible to the system.
  • Threats only "exist" when inscribed: MITRE's CVE database doesn't catalog pre-existing vulnerabilities—it produces vulnerabilities as objects of knowledge and intervention.
  • Security only "exists" through writing: Protocols, standards, certifications, clearances—all written before any "secure" thing can be recognized as such.
There is no outside: You cannot resist by being "authentic" or "present" or "unmediated," because those concepts only make sense within the system of traces. The cybernetic system doesn't represent reality—it is the condition of reality being intelligible at all.

Différance: Power in the Delay

Derrida's neologism différance (difference + deferral) describes how meaning works: it's always deferred through chains of signifiers, never fully present.

MITRE's power operates through this structure:

The CVE Example

  1. A vulnerability is assigned a CVE number before anyone fully understands it
  2. The number circulates through security teams, vendors, compliance frameworks
  3. Each use defers meaning to other uses: "CVE-2024-12345 requires patching per NIST guidelines per our vendor SLA per federal mandate"
  4. No one entity controls the meaning, but the system as a whole determines what counts as secure
  5. By the time you "understand" the vulnerability, you're already operating within MITRE's framework

This is power without sovereignty—not a dictator giving orders, but a system of inscription that makes certain thoughts thinkable and others literally unthinkable.

Supplementarity: The Parasite as Host

Derrida's concept of the supplement is devastating for understanding how cybernetic systems colonize life:

A supplement is supposed to be:

  • External—added from outside
  • Optional—enhancing something already complete
  • Secondary—dependent on what it supplements

But supplements always reveal that what they "supplement" was incomplete, dependent, secondary all along.

MITRE as Supplement

MITRE is presented as supplementing government:

  • "We just provide technical advice" (external)
  • "Government could function without us" (optional)
  • "We serve the sponsor's mission" (secondary)

But look what happens:

  • Air traffic control: "Supplementary" systems become the infrastructure without which planes fall from the sky
  • Cybersecurity: "Advisory" frameworks become the mandatory standards that determine what counts as secure
  • Intelligence: "Technical support" becomes the analytical framework that determines what counts as a threat
The supplement becomes the origin: MITRE doesn't serve government—government has become the name for the network of technical systems that MITRE-type entities operate. The "supplement" was the real infrastructure all along.

Logocentrism: The Human Decision Myth

Western metaphysics is logocentric—it privileges the logos (speech, reason, presence, the human). Derrida shows this was always a fantasy sustained by forgetting the material systems (writing, inscription, trace) that make "presence" possible.

The cybernetic war machine makes this undeniable:

  • "Humans in the loop": Operators can press the button, but the loop—sensing, processing, targeting—is automated. The "human decision" is just ratifying what the system has already determined.
  • "Human judgment": Analysts review MITRE threat models, but their "judgment" consists of applying MITRE-defined categories. There's no standpoint outside the framework from which to judge.
  • "Democratic oversight": Congress can approve budgets, but the technical specifications were written by engineers with clearances applying standards that were defined decades ago. "Oversight" happens within parameters set by the system.

Derrida's point: This isn't a recent degradation of human agency. There was never a "human decision" prior to the systems of inscription that make decision-making legible. We've always been cyborgs; cybernetics just makes it impossible to pretend otherwise.

Deconstruction as Method: What Can We Do?

Derrida is often misread as nihilistic—if everything is text/trace/différance, doesn't that mean nothing matters? No. It means we need different tactics.

Deconstruction shows:

  1. The system isn't total: Every attempt at closure (MITRE defining "all" threats) produces remainders, exceptions, anomalies
  2. Contradictions are structural: MITRE claims to be "non-profit" while operating as permanent infrastructure—this isn't hypocrisy, it's how the system reproduces itself
  3. We can't get outside: But we can work the seams, exploit the gaps, read against the grain

Tactical implications:

  • Trace their traces: MITRE claims neutrality—catalog every instance where their "technical" choices encode political decisions
  • Expose supplementarity: Show how the "optional" advisory became mandatory infrastructure—force them to acknowledge their power
  • Proliferate alternatives: MITRE built the CVE ontology—build other ontologies, make their categorization visible as one choice among many
  • Demand legibility: Classification hides their operation—force disclosure, FOIA their communications, make the inscriptions visible
Derrida's warning: You cannot defeat the system by appealing to "human values" against "machines," because that opposition was always part of how the system operates. The task is to make visible the violence of the program—the decisions written into infrastructure that foreclose other futures before anyone can deliberate about them.
